How to Check a Car's VIN in India — Complete Guide

Published on Jan 1, 2025 • 6 min read

The Vehicle Identification Number, commonly known as the VIN or chassis number, is the fingerprint of a car. Just like human fingerprints, no two vehicles in operation possess the same VIN. Comprising 17 alphanumeric characters, this sequence holds a vast amount of critical information about the vehicle, including its manufacturer, year of production, plant of assembly, and unique serial number. When buying a used car in India, failing to thoroughly verify the VIN can lead you directly into a financial nightmare—buying a stolen vehicle, a car written off in a flood, or one built from the salvaged parts of multiple accidents. This comprehensive guide will teach you exactly how to find, decode, and verify a car's VIN in India to ensure your purchase is safe and legitimate.

Where to Find the VIN on a Car

Before you can decode or verify the VIN, you need to locate it physically on the car. Manufacturers stamp the VIN in multiple locations to prevent tampering and ensure identification is possible even if parts are replaced. The most common locations include:

  • The Dashboard / Windshield: Look through the bottom corner of the windshield on the driver's side. Most modern cars have a small metal plate visible from the outside.
  • The Driver's Side Door Jamb: Open the driver’s side door and look for a sticker or metal placard on the door pillar where the door latches. This sticker often includes tire pressure information and the manufacturing date as well.
  • Under the Hood (Engine Bay): The VIN is commonly stamped directly onto the vehicle's chassis or firewall inside the engine compartment. It is deeply engraved to prevent easy removal.
  • Under the Front Seats: On many cars (like those from Maruti Suzuki or Hyundai), a flap in the carpet underneath the driver or passenger seat conceals the stamped chassis number.
  • The Registration Certificate (RC): The VIN is officially recorded on the car's RC book or smart card under the "Chassis No" section.

Pro Tip: Always verify that the VIN stamped on the metal frame matches the one on the dashboard, the door sticker, and the RC book. Even a single mismatched character is a major red flag.

How to Decode a 17-Character VIN

Decoding the 17 characters of a VIN gives you the exact blueprint of the vehicle. The standardized format works as follows:

  • Characters 1-3 (WMI): World Manufacturer Identifier. It identifies the country of origin and the manufacturer. For example, 'MA' often indicates India.
  • Characters 4-8 (VDS): Vehicle Descriptor Section. These characters describe the vehicle model, body style, engine type, and transmission.
  • Character 9: The Check Digit. This is a security feature—a mathematical calculation based on the other characters to detect invalid or tampered VINs.
  • Character 10: The Model Year. This letter or number represents the specific year the car was manufactured (e.g., 'M' for 2021, 'N' for 2022). This is critical for catching sellers who claim the car is newer than it actually is.
  • Character 11: The Assembly Plant. Identifies which factory built the vehicle.
  • Characters 12-17 (VIS): Vehicle Identifier Section. The unique serial number assigned to the specific car as it rolled off the production line.

Verifying the VIN via Vahan.gov.in

In India, the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways maintains a centralized database called Vahan. You can use this free portal to verify a vehicle's details against the provided VIN. By entering the car's registration number on the Vahan Parivahan website or app, you can view masked details of the chassis number and engine number. While the last few digits are often masked for privacy, you can match the visible portions against the physical VIN on the car. Vahan also provides crucial data such as active challans, blacklisted status, fitness validity, and exact owner details. If a car is marked as stolen or blacklisted by the RTO, the Vahan portal will display this immediately.

Cross-Checking the VIN and Engine Number

The VIN (Chassis Number) and the Engine Number are a pair. The RC book lists both. When inspecting a used car, you must physically locate both numbers on the vehicle and cross-reference them with the documents. Engine numbers are stamped onto the engine block, though they can be hard to see without a flashlight and sometimes a mirror. If the chassis number matches the RC, but the engine number does not, it means the car's engine has been replaced. While an engine swap isn't inherently illegal if endorsed by the RTO, an unendorsed engine change is illegal and a sign that the car might have had severe mechanical failure or the engine is stolen.

What a Mismatched or Tampered VIN Means

Finding inconsistencies with the VIN is the ultimate dealbreaker. If the VIN looks scratched, altered, or welded over, it is highly probable that the car is stolen, or it is a "cut-and-shut" job where two severely damaged cars have been welded together to make one functioning vehicle. Sometimes, scammers will transfer the VIN plates from a legally scrapped car onto a stolen identical model (a process known as cloning). If the fonts look misaligned, the metal shows signs of grinding, or the rivets holding the VIN plate look non-factory, walk away immediately and consider reporting the vehicle to the authorities.
