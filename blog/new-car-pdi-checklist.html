New Car PDI Checklist: 30 Things to Check Before Taking Delivery

Published on Jan 1, 2025 • 6 min read

Taking delivery of a new car is a momentous occasion. It’s easy to get swept up in the excitement, the ribbon-cutting, and the ceremonial handover of the keys. However, before you drive off the lot and make the purchase final, it is absolutely essential to perform a Pre-Delivery Inspection (PDI). A PDI ensures that the vehicle you are taking home is in the exact pristine condition promised by the manufacturer.

To help you navigate this crucial step, we have compiled the ultimate new car PDI checklist. Print this list, take it to the dealership, and take your time examining every detail. Do not let the sales staff rush you.

Pro Tip: Always insist on doing the PDI during the daytime in an open yard. Dealership lighting can easily mask paint imperfections, swirl marks, and minor dents.

Exterior Inspection

The exterior is the most prone to transit damage. Examine it meticulously from all angles.

  1. Paint Quality: Look closely for scratches, swirl marks, dents, or uneven paint finishes. Check the roof and lower bumpers specifically.
  2. Panel Gaps: Ensure consistent spacing between the doors, bonnet, boot, and fenders. Uneven gaps suggest manufacturing defects or post-factory repairs.
  3. Glass and Windshield: Inspect all windows, the windshield, and the rear glass for any chips, cracks, or deep scratches.
  4. Headlights and Taillights: Check the housings for condensation or moisture inside. Ensure they fit flush with the body panels.
  5. Tires: Verify the manufacturing date of the tires (it should be recent). Check for any sidewall bulges, cuts, or uneven wear. Ensure all tires are of the same brand and specification.
  6. Alloy Wheels/Hubcaps: Look for scuff marks or curb damage that might have occurred during maneuvering at the stockyard.
  7. Doors, Boot, and Bonnet: Open and close all doors, the boot, and the bonnet multiple times. They should open smoothly and latch securely without excessive force.
  8. Underbody: Crouch down and check underneath for any hanging wires, fluid leaks, or early signs of rust on the exhaust pipe or suspension components.

Interior and Electricals

The cabin is where you’ll spend your time. Verify that everything works perfectly and looks pristine.

  1. Odometer Reading: A new car should generally have less than 50-100 kilometers on the odometer, accounting for factory testing and transit.
  2. Upholstery and Seats: Check the fabric or leather for tears, stains, or loose stitching. Ensure seat adjustments (manual or powered) work across their full range.
  3. Dashboard and Trim: Inspect the plastics and trim pieces for scratches, proper fitment, and any rattles when tapping them lightly.
  4. Air Conditioning (AC): Turn the AC to the maximum cold setting. It should blow ice-cold air within a minute. Test the heater and all vent directional controls.
  5. Infotainment System: Test the touchscreen responsiveness. Connect your phone via Bluetooth/Apple CarPlay/Android Auto. Test the speakers for distortion.
  6. All Windows and Sunroof: Roll all windows up and down. If equipped, open and close the sunroof fully to check for smooth, quiet operation.
  7. Wipers and Washers: Test the front and rear windshield wipers at all speeds. Ensure the washer fluid sprays correctly.
  8. Lights and Signals: Turn on the headlights (high and low beam), fog lamps, turn indicators, hazard lights, and cabin lights. Verify they all work.
  9. Horn: Press the horn to ensure it sounds clear and strong.
  10. Mirrors: Adjust the internal rearview mirror and both exterior wing mirrors. Test power-folding mirrors if applicable.
  11. Ports and Outlets: Plug a device into all USB ports and the 12V socket to ensure they provide power.

Engine Bay and Mechanicals

You don't need to be a mechanic, but you should verify the basics under the hood.

  1. Engine Bay Cleanliness: The engine bay should be clean, dust-free, and look brand new, without any oil spills or grime.
  2. Fluid Levels: Check the engine oil dipstick (should be golden brown and full), coolant reservoir, and brake fluid levels. They must be at the 'Max' mark.
  3. Battery: Ensure the battery terminals are clean, tight, and free of any white corrosion. Check the battery manufacturing date.
  4. Engine Start: Start the engine cold. It should crank quickly and settle into a smooth, quiet idle without any heavy vibrations or knocking sounds.
  5. Exhaust: Check the exhaust pipe for excessive black soot or unusual colored smoke during startup.

Boot, Accessories, and Documents

Finally, ensure you are getting everything you paid for and that the paperwork is perfect.

  1. Spare Wheel: Open the boot and verify the presence of the spare wheel. Ensure it is fully inflated and secured properly.
  2. Toolkit and Jack: Check that the standard factory toolkit, jack, and warning triangle are present in their designated compartments.
  3. Accessories: Cross-check the invoice against physical accessories installed (e.g., floor mats, mud flaps, seat covers). Ensure they are fitted correctly.
  4. Keys: You must receive at least two sets of keys (smart keys or manual). Test both keys to ensure they lock, unlock, and start the vehicle.
  5. VIN Verification: Match the Vehicle Identification Number (VIN) stamped on the car's chassis (usually near the windshield or driver's door jamb) with the VIN on the invoice and insurance papers.
  6. Documentation: Ensure you have the original tax receipt, temporary or permanent registration certificate (RC), valid insurance policy, owner’s manual, and warranty booklets fully stamped.

By diligently following this new car PDI checklist, you protect your investment and ensure that the beginning of your journey with your new vehicle is as perfect as you imagined. Never let the excitement of delivery day overshadow the necessity of a thorough inspection.
