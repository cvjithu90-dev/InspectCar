How to Check Car Service History Before Buying — A Complete Guide

Published on Jan 1, 2025 • 5 min read

When you are in the market for a used car, the vehicle's exterior shine and pristine interior might easily seduce you. However, the true story of a car's health lies in its past—specifically, its service history. A car's service history is analogous to a person's medical record; it tells you how well the car has been treated, what major procedures it has undergone, and whether it has been neglected.

Understanding how to check car service history is arguably one of the most critical steps in the pre-purchase process. In this guide, we break down why it matters, how to interpret the records, and the red flags you must absolutely avoid.

Why Car Service History Matters

A comprehensive service history provides peace of mind and undeniable proof that the previous owner cared for the vehicle. Regular oil changes, timely fluid top-ups, and scheduled part replacements drastically reduce the likelihood of sudden mechanical failure. Furthermore, if you are purchasing a car that is still under the manufacturer's warranty, that warranty is only valid if the car has been serviced according to the official schedule at authorized centers. Finally, a documented service history significantly boosts the car's resale value, making it a smarter long-term investment for you.

How to Read and Verify a Service Book

Check for Consistent Intervals

Every car comes with an owner's manual detailing the recommended service intervals—usually every 10,000 km or 1 year, whichever comes first. Open the service book and look at the stamps. Ensure that the dates and odometer readings progress logically and align with the manufacturer's guidelines. A car driven 50,000 km should ideally have 5 regular service stamps.

Cross-Verify with Dealer Records

Unfortunately, fake service stamps are not uncommon in the used car market. Unscrupulous sellers can easily acquire fake rubber stamps to forge a pristine history.

Pro Tip: Don't just look at the stamps. Call the authorized service center whose stamp is in the book. Quote the car's registration number or VIN (Vehicle Identification Number), and ask them to verify the last recorded mileage and service date. Most dealerships are happy to provide this information.

Look for Major Part Replacements

At certain mileage milestones (e.g., 60,000 km or 100,000 km), cars require major servicing. This often includes replacing the timing belt or chain, flushing the transmission fluid, or changing spark plugs. Ensure these major (and expensive) services have been completed; otherwise, you'll be footing the bill shortly after purchase.

OEM Authorized Centers vs. Independent Garages

A "Full Service History" (FSH) from OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer) authorized centers is the gold standard. It guarantees that genuine parts were used and factory-trained technicians handled the vehicle. However, older cars are often serviced at independent garages to save costs. While this isn't necessarily a dealbreaker, you should ask the seller for detailed invoices from the independent garage to verify what parts were used and what work was actually done. Receipts detailing brand-name oil and quality aftermarket parts are a good sign.

What Missing Service Stamps Mean

Skipped Services and Mechanical Wear

If you notice a gap of two years or 25,000 km between services, it means the car ran on degraded engine oil for far too long. Old oil loses its lubricating properties and turns into sludge, which causes accelerated wear on the engine's internal moving parts. This kind of neglect can drastically shorten the lifespan of the engine.

Timing Chain and Gearbox Risks

Skipping major services can lead to catastrophic failures. For instance, if a timing belt snaps because it wasn't replaced at the recommended interval, it can destroy the engine's valves and pistons, resulting in a repair bill that could easily run into lakhs of rupees. Similarly, ignoring automatic transmission fluid changes can lead to a ruined gearbox.

Correlating OBD Diagnostics with Service Gaps

Sometimes, the car's computer tells the story the service book is hiding. A professional OBD (On-Board Diagnostics) scan can reveal historical fault codes that were cleared but not properly addressed. If the service book is empty but the OBD scanner shows a history of recurrent misfires, sensor failures, or overheating events, you are looking at a neglected vehicle that has been poorly maintained.
