20 Things to Check Before Buying a Second-Hand Car in India

Published on Jan 1, 2025 • 5 min read

Purchasing a pre-owned vehicle can be a fantastic financial decision, allowing you to bypass the steep depreciation that new cars experience in their first few years. However, the used car market in India is largely unorganized, making it a minefield for the uninformed buyer. Knowing the essential things to check before buying a second-hand car is your best defense against ending up with a money pit.

To help you navigate this process with confidence, we've compiled an exhaustive, step-by-step checklist. Whether you're eyeing a budget hatchback for city commutes or a premium SUV for highway cruising, here are the 20 crucial things you must inspect before finalizing your purchase.

Document & Paperwork Verification

1. Registration Certificate (RC) Transferability

The RC is the most critical document. Ensure the seller is the actual owner listed on the RC. Check the number of previous owners; a car that has changed hands multiple times in a short period is a major red flag. If the car is from another state, ensure a valid NOC is available.

2. Valid Insurance Policy

Check if the car has a comprehensive or third-party insurance policy. A comprehensive policy with a substantial No Claim Bonus (NCB) usually indicates that the car hasn't been in a major accident recently. Verify the engine and chassis number on the policy match the RC.

3. Pollution Under Control (PUC) Certificate

A valid PUC is mandatory by law in India. A missing PUC could indicate that the car has underlying engine issues or excessive emissions that prevent it from passing the test.

4. Comprehensive Service Book

A stamped service book from an authorized service center proves the car was maintained according to the manufacturer's recommendations. Look for regular oil changes and timely replacement of major components like the timing belt.

Pro Tip: Call the authorized service center mentioned in the service book and quote the car's registration number. They can often verbally verify the service history and genuine mileage.

Exterior & Structural Inspection

5. Paint Condition and Consistency

Inspect the car in bright daylight. Look closely at the paint on adjacent panels. If the fender is slightly shinier or a different shade than the door, it has been repainted, likely due to an accident.

6. Panel Gaps and Alignment

Examine the gaps between the doors, hood, and trunk. Factory assembly results in uniform gaps. Uneven or wide gaps indicate poor bodywork repairs after a collision.

7. Signs of Rust and Corrosion

In humid coastal regions of India, rust is a car's worst enemy. Check the wheel arches, the bottom edges of the doors, and under the floor mats. Blistering paint is often the first sign of rust underneath.

8. Window and Windshield Glass

Every piece of glass on the car should ideally have the same manufacturing year printed on it. A mismatched date on the windshield or a side window means it was replaced.

Under the Hood: Engine & Mechanics

9. Engine Oil Quality

Pull the dipstick and check the oil. It shouldn't be gritty, sludgy, or look like a milky emulsion. The latter indicates a blown head gasket, which is an extremely expensive repair.

10. Coolant Condition

The coolant should be a clear, bright color (green, pink, or orange). If it looks rusty or has oil floating in it, there are severe internal engine problems.

11. Battery Health and Terminals

Check the battery's manufacturing date. Most car batteries in India last 3-4 years. Look for heavy white corrosion on the terminals, which indicates poor maintenance.

12. Exhaust Smoke Colors

Start the car and observe the exhaust. Continuous white smoke indicates burning coolant, blue smoke means the engine is burning oil, and black smoke suggests fuel mixture issues.

Interior & Electronics Check

13. Odometer Verification

Odometer tampering is rampant in India. Does a car with only 30,000 km have heavily worn-out pedals, a shiny steering wheel, and saggy seats? If the physical wear doesn't match the low mileage, walk away.

14. Air Conditioning Performance

Turn the AC on full blast. It should blow cold air within a minute. Listen for any loud hissing or clanking noises from the AC compressor under the hood.

15. Electricals and Dashboard Lights

Test every single button, window switch, wiper, and light. When you turn the ignition on, all warning lights (Check Engine, ABS, Airbag) should illuminate and then turn off once the engine starts.

16. Signs of Flood Damage

Smell the interior for a musty, damp odor. Check under the carpets and inside the spare tire well in the boot for mud residue or waterline marks. Flood-damaged cars will have endless electrical gremlins.

The Test Drive & Diagnostics

17. Suspension and Steering Feel

Drive over rough roads to test the suspension. It shouldn't clunk or bounce excessively. The steering should feel tight and responsive, not loose or vibrating at higher speeds.

18. Gearbox and Clutch Operation

The gears should slot in smoothly without resistance or grinding. If the clutch pedal is extremely hard or the car revs without accelerating (slipping clutch), expect a hefty repair bill soon.

19. Braking Efficiency

At a safe speed, brake firmly. The car should stop in a straight line. If the steering wheel vibrates violently during braking, the brake rotors are warped.

20. OBD Diagnostic Scan

Finally, connect an OBD2 scanner to check for hidden fault codes. Sellers often clear warning lights temporarily, but a scanner will reveal the true electronic history and pending faults of the vehicle.
