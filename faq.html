What's Covered in the Inspection

7 Questions

InspectCar's 300+ point inspection covers: Exterior body panels and paint (including paint gauge test), engine and transmission performance, OBD diagnostic scan for fault codes, interior condition and all electrical systems, tyre condition and tread depth, underbody inspection for rust and leaks, suspension and steering, AC performance, and document verification including RC, insurance, and PUC.

Yes. The engine check includes: visual inspection of the engine bay for leaks, oil condition check, coolant level and condition, cold start test for abnormal smoke or noise, idle RPM check, transmission fluid check, and a full OBD scan reading all engine-related fault codes and live sensor data.

A paint thickness gauge uses an electromagnetic sensor to measure the paint coat depth on each body panel in microns. Original factory paint is 90–130 microns thick. A panel that has been repainted after accident repair shows 160–300+ microns. This test reveals hidden accident history that looks perfectly normal to the naked eye — it is one of the most important tools in a used car inspection.

Yes. Tyre inspection includes: tread depth measurement on all 4 tyres, check for uneven wear patterns (indicating alignment or suspension problems), sidewall damage, tyre age verification using the DOT manufacturing code (tyres over 5 years old should be replaced regardless of tread), spare tyre condition, and wheel rim condition.

Yes. The underbody inspection checks for: chassis rust or structural corrosion, evidence of major repairs or welding (indicating accident frame damage), fluid leaks from the engine, gearbox, or differential, exhaust system condition, suspension arm and bush condition, and any impact damage from driving over large obstacles.

All electrical systems are tested including: battery health (CCA testing), alternator output, all exterior lights (headlights, tail lights, indicators, DRLs, fog lights), interior lights, AC compressor and blower, all power windows, central locking, horn, reverse camera or sensors, USB ports, infotainment system, and any OBD-reported electrical fault codes.

Yes. Document verification includes: checking that the RC book matches the vehicle's chassis number and engine number, verifying insurance validity, checking PUC certificate validity, checking for hypothecation endorsement (active loan), verifying the odometer reading against the service book entries, and confirming the vehicle is not under any court order or blacklisted.

Accident damage is detected through: paint thickness gauge readings (repainted panels), panel gap measurements (misaligned body panels after repair), OBD fault codes related to airbag or ABS systems, visual inspection of the firewall and chassis for repair welds, and underbody inspection for bent or repaired structural members.

Odometer tampering is detected by cross-referencing the displayed reading with: physical wear indicators (accelerator pedal rubber, driver seat fabric, steering wheel wear), service book stamp dates and km entries, OBD ECU total odometer memory (in some vehicles), and tyre age vs claimed mileage.

Flood damage indicators include: musty or moldy smell inside the cabin, waterline marks visible under the seats or in the boot, corrosion on fuse box terminals and connectors, mud residue in the spare tyre well or under the carpet, sticky or seized door hinges, condensation inside headlamps, and corroded or replaced electrical connectors.

InspectCar verifies the RC book for hypothecation (loan) endorsement. If a lender (bank/NBFC) is listed on the RC, the vehicle has an active loan. The seller cannot legally transfer ownership without an NOC from the lender. We advise buyers to demand Form 35 (NOC) and check the Parivahan portal for this verification.

New Car PDI — What's Checked

7 Questions

InspectCar's 300+ point new car PDI covers: all body panels and paint (paint gauge test on every panel), all glass panes for OEM markings and chips, all electrical systems and infotainment, AC performance, OBD scan for pre-existing fault codes, all fluid levels, tyre condition and DOT code, underbody for transit damage, and complete documentation and accessory verification.

Yes. Factory paint defects include orange peel texture, paint runs, dirt inclusions, fish-eye marks, and uneven coverage. Transit handling can cause bumper or panel paint damage that dealers repair before delivery without disclosure. A paint gauge test detects all of these.

Stockyard damage occurs when cars are stored in dealer lots for weeks or months awaiting buyers. During this time, rats can chew wiring harnesses, battery voltage drops affecting ECU, UV exposure fades plastic trim, and hailstorms can cause small dents. InspectCar's PDI specifically inspects for all stockyard damage types.

Signs of a demo car include: odometer reading above 200 km, wear on driver's accelerator pedal rubber, scuffs on door sill entry plates, previous service history stamp in the service book, fuel level below full, and any interior wear not consistent with a brand new car.

Verify: Form 21 (Notice of dispatch from manufacturer), Form 22 (Roadworthiness certificate), manufacturer's invoice (check all accessories match), warranty booklet, owner's manual, insurance policy, 2 sets of keys, spare tyre with matching OEM size, wheel spanner and jack, and all accessories listed on your booking invoice.

Yes. Rat bite damage to wiring harnesses is a genuine problem in Bangalore dealership stockyards. Our PDI specifically inspects the engine bay wiring harness, underbody wiring, and runs a full electrical function test. If any electrical fault is found, we run an OBD scan to identify affected circuits.

Even brand new cars can have pre-existing OBD fault codes from: factory calibration runs, transport handling, battery drain during storage, or ECU software issues. InspectCar runs a full OBD scan on every new car PDI. Any stored or pending DTCs are documented in the report so you can demand they be cleared and verified before taking delivery.
